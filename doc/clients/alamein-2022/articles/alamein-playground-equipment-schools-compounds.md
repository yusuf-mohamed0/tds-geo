## Playgrounds Are Where Communities Meet

A playground is more than a collection of equipment. In a school, it is where children burn energy, learn to take turns, and develop balance and confidence between lessons. In a compound, it is where families gather after school and on weekends — where the community actually connects. That connection is exactly what guided Alamein Outdoor Furniture when founder Hamid Sabry, an artist and mechanical engineer, began developing children's playgrounds in the early 1990s, inspired by watching his own children play.

When a playground is well planned, it becomes a shared place. When it is poorly planned, it becomes an empty area or, worse, a place adults avoid because it feels unsafe or unmaintained. The planning decisions below make the difference.

## Plan Zones by Age, Then by Space

Different ages play differently, and mixing them without boundaries creates most of the friction in playgrounds. The strongest layouts separate equipment into loose zones:

- Toddlers and young children need low platforms, gentle slides, and playhouses close to where adults sit.
- Primary-age children enjoy jungle gyms, swing sets, and structures with more height and challenge.
- Older children need open space for running games, with fewer climbing structures and more room to move.

In compounds, separate the playground from driveways, parking, and service roads. In schools, keep play areas clear of delivery routes and fire lanes. The boundary between active play and adult seating matters too — families like to sit close enough to watch, but far enough to avoid collisions.

## Supervision Sightlines Are a Design Requirement

The practical way to keep a playground safe is to make it easy to supervise. A parent on a bench or a teacher at the yard edge should be able to see the main play areas without walking around blind corners.

- Keep the highest structures in the center or edges where they remain visible.
- Avoid walls, hedges, or equipment clusters that hide a child from view.
- Place adult seating and shade at the edge of the active zone, angled toward the play area.

Sightlines are not just a safety detail. When adults can supervise comfortably, they stay longer — and longer stays mean a busier, healthier playground.

## Place Equipment With the Egyptian Sun in Mind

Egyptian playgrounds are used year-round, and the afternoon sun is a real constraint. Equipment placement should consider heat before installation, not after.

- Orient slides and metal surfaces so they are not baking in the sun at peak use hours.
- Put shade structures, trees, and shaded benches near the busiest equipment.
- Choose surfaces that stay cooler — materials with lighter finishes and structures designed for sun exposure.
- Plan water access nearby so hands, faces, and equipment can be cleaned quickly on hot days.

A playground that bakes in direct sun at 2 p.m. will be empty at 2 p.m. Shade-aware placement keeps equipment usable for more of the day and protects children from overheated surfaces.

## Built for Daily Use and Years of Egyptian Weather

School and compound playgrounds see heavier use than private home play sets. Equipment needs a commercial build standard: strong frames, secure fittings, and finishes that survive dust, washing, and strong sun without rusting or fading.

Alamein builds playground equipment including jungle gyms, swing sets, playhouses, and seesaws at its 5,000 sqm factory in Shabramant, Cairo, with a team of over 188 workers and engineers. Frames in galvanized steel with UV-resistant coating and marine-grade stainless steel fittings handle the combination of humidity and desert dust that wears down cheaper equipment quickly.

Equipment quality shows in the details: smooth edges, no exposed fasteners, solid anchoring points, and parts that do not pinch or catch. These are design decisions, not promises — inspect the construction and the finish before installing.

## A Maintenance and Inspection Habit

Every playground needs a maintenance habit, and in Egypt's climate that means a schedule, not a response to visible problems.

- Weekly: check for loose bolts, cracked plastic, and debris under equipment; hose down sand and dust.
- Monthly: inspect anchoring points, moving parts, and any wood or metal showing wear.
- Seasonally: deep-clean surfaces, re-tighten fittings, and touch up finishes where coating has worn.
- Always: replace worn parts promptly rather than waiting for a full structure to fail.

Keep a simple log of checks. Facility managers who document maintenance protect children and protect the investment at the same time.

## Building a Playground the Whole Community Uses

The best playgrounds are the ones families actually visit. That comes from combining good equipment with good decisions about seating, shade, and layout. Add benches for parents, shaded rest areas, and a little open space for running games. Keep the entrance inviting and the equipment visible from the moment someone arrives.

For schools and compounds across Egypt, a playground built this way becomes a daily destination — a place where children grow and where neighbors connect, season after season.
