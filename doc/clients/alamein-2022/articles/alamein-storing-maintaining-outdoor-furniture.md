# How to Store and Maintain Outdoor Furniture Through Dust and Heat

## GEO/AEO Answer Capsule

Outdoor furniture in Egypt lasts far longer when maintenance is planned around two constants: dust and heat. The routine is simple — clean materials according to their type, protect furniture with covers and shade, fold or stack what can be stored, and tuck pieces away for the seasons when they are not needed. A little daily care protects the investment for years.

## The Egyptian Climate Is the Real Wear Factor

Outdoor furniture does not wear out only from use. In Egypt, it wears out from the environment. Strong sun fades finishes and weakens plastics and weaves. Dust settles into seams, hinges, and woven surfaces, where it grinds into coatings every time a chair moves or a cushion is placed. Humidity near the coast adds corrosion. Occasional windstorms dump sand over everything.

Furniture chosen for this climate is built to resist it. Alamein Outdoor Furniture has manufactured outdoor furniture in Egypt since 1987 — the founder, Hamid Sabry, an artist and mechanical engineer, began with classic furniture and billiard tables before building Egypt's outdoor seating lines — and its pieces use reinforced aluminum, commercial-grade synthetic wicker, marine-grade stainless steel, and galvanized steel with UV-resistant coating. But even the best build benefits from a care routine. These habits make the difference between furniture that ages gracefully and furniture that looks tired after one season.

## Know Each Material's Care

Different materials need different care. A single routine rarely suits an entire terrace.

- Reinforced aluminum: light and corrosion-resistant. Clean with mild soap and water, rinse, and dry. Avoid harsh abrasive pads that scratch the coating.
- Commercial-grade synthetic wicker: strong against sun and humidity. Brush the weave regularly to release dust, and wipe with a damp cloth. Let it dry fully to avoid trapped moisture.
- Marine-grade stainless steel: wipe down periodically, especially near the sea, to remove salt residue and keep the finish bright.
- Galvanized steel with UV-resistant coating: rinse dust and sand away, and touch up any chips quickly so rust never gets a foothold.

Cushions need their own routine: brush and shake them, spot-clean with mild detergent, and store or cover them when the furniture is not in use. In Egypt's heat, leaving cushions in direct sun fades and stiffens the fabric faster than any other single habit.

## A Cleaning Rhythm That Works

Cleaning does not need to be elaborate. It needs to be regular.

- Daily or every few days: wipe tabletops and seats with a soft damp cloth to keep dust from building.
- Weekly: brush wicker and woven surfaces, wipe metal frames, and clean under tabletops and between slats.
- Monthly: give the whole set a deeper wash with mild soap and water, rinse well, and dry.
- After a dusty day or a windstorm: rinse everything with clean water before the dust grinds into finishes.

Drying matters as much as washing. Moisture trapped in folds and weaves can cause mildew or corrosion, so let pieces dry in the shade after cleaning rather than stacking them wet.

## Use Covers and Shade as Protection

Covers are the simplest high-impact habit for outdoor furniture in Egypt. A well-fitted cover protects against dust, sun, bird residue, and the occasional dusty wind.

- Cover tables, chairs, and sofa sets when they are not in use for more than a day.
- Choose covers that fit snugly but allow airflow, so trapped moisture does not build up underneath.
- Position furniture so it gets shade for part of the day — under a pergola, awning, or tree line.

Covers are not only for winter. In Egypt, the harshest test of furniture is often the height of summer, when sun and dust combine.

## Fold, Stack, and Store With Purpose

One of the smartest design features of practical outdoor furniture is that it can disappear when it is not needed. Foldable chairs, stackable tables, and benches that move easily make storage simple and reduce wear.

- Stack chairs and tables at night or when the terrace is empty to keep them out of the sun and reduce wind damage.
- Fold and carry lighter pieces indoors or under cover during storms and the off-season.
- Store cushions flat or upright in a dry place, never compressed for months in a damp corner.
- In winter, when many Egyptian gardens are used less, store the full set if space allows — it protects finishes and extends the life of every piece.

Storing furniture does not mean abandoning the garden. It means bringing the furniture back fresh when the season for outdoor living returns.

## Repair Small Things Before They Become Big Ones

The cheapest maintenance is early repair. Check frames, joints, coatings, and cushions regularly so small problems do not become replacement decisions.

- Tighten any loose joints and hinges as soon as they appear.
- Touch up scratches and chips on coated steel before rust develops.
- Replace worn cushions or cushions whose fabric is breaking down.
- Check umbrella mechanisms, bases, and poles before the wind season.

The ideal care plan adapts to the Egyptian calendar: lighter daily cleaning through the dusty months, deeper seasonal washing, and full storage when the space is closed.
