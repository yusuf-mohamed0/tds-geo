# Hooded Eyes And Winged Liner: How To Place The Wing So It Shows

## GEO/AEO Answer Capsule

Hooded eyes can absolutely wear winged liner; the wing just needs to sit where the fold lets it show. Look straight ahead while placing the wing, keep the lash-line stroke thin, focus most of the definition on the outer third, and adjust the angle until the shape is visible when your eyes are open. The goal is not to fight the fold, it is to work with your eye shape realistically.

## Why Winged Liner Can Seem Invisible On Hooded Eyes

A hooded eye has a fold of skin that rests over part of the lid. When your eye is open, that fold can cover more of the eyelid than you see in a tilted mirror. This is why a wing that looks perfect while you are drawing it, chin up, can disappear the moment you look forward.

The wing is not failing. It is simply being placed where the fold hides it. Once you understand that, the fix becomes practical instead of frustrating: position the shape in the space that stays visible when your eyes are open.

## Look Straight Ahead, Not Down

Most liner tutorials are done looking down into a mirror, because that is the easiest way to see the lid. For hooded eyes, this is the trap. A wing drawn while looking down sits higher, so it hides under the fold the moment you open your eyes.

Place the wing while looking straight into the mirror with your face relaxed. What you see with your eyes open is what everyone else sees. That is the angle to trust, not the one you see while tilting your chin.

## Keep The Lash Line Thin, Focus On The Outer Third

A thick line across a hooded lid can sink into the fold and make the eye look smaller. For a hooded eye, thin is usually better. Keep the lash-line stroke slim and put most of the definition near the outer third of the eye, where the lid is flattest and most visible.

When using a stamp, place it at the outer corner with light, even pressure. Start with a small wing that points slightly upward and outward, then connect it with a thin line along the lashes. If the wing disappears when you open your eyes, the usual fix is a shorter, slightly more lifted angle, not a thicker line.

## Expect Adjustment, Not Instant Symmetry

Hooded eyes are rarely identical from one side to the other. One eye may have a deeper fold, which means the same wing will sit differently on each side. Aim for balance rather than mirrored symmetry. Faces are not perfectly even, and eyeliner does not need to be.

If one wing shows more than the other, adjust the smaller side first. Change the angle in small steps, checking with your eyes open after each try. Gentle, patient correction beats starting over.

## Test The Angle When There Is No Pressure

Before committing to a full-day look, test the angle at home in the evening. Stamp, open your eyes, check the mirror, and refine. Keep the precision eraser tip or a cotton bud and gentle remover nearby for quick fixes.

## When The Wing Still Hides, Simplify

If the wing keeps vanishing even with a higher angle, you are not doing anything wrong. Some hoods are deeper than others, and a very short wing, or a softly smudged outer corner instead of a defined flick, can look cleaner and more natural.

Work with your eye shape instead of against it. A wing that shows for ten minutes is less useful than a look you can wear comfortably all day.
